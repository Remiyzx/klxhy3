How to Say "2" in Chinese? (èr vs liǎng)

2
èr
  • Use "èr" when referring to the number itself.
  • Counting sequence: yī, èr, sān...
  • Ordinal numbers: èr céng (二层 - second floor).
2
liǎng
  • Use "liǎng" when indicating a specific quantity that requires a measure word.
  • Example: liǎng ge rén (两个人 - two people). "ge" is a measure word!

Note on 10000 (wàn / 万):
In standard Mandarin, wàn (万) serves as a primary base unit for 10000 (e.g. 10000 = yī wàn). While English and Malay count in thousands ("ten thousand" / "sepuluh ribu"), Chinese grouping shifts every 4 zeros (万). Non-native speakers sometimes casually say shí qiān under English/Malay or dialect influence, but in standard Mandarin wàn must be used!

4. Hundreds & Zeros Reading Rules KLXHY pg 15

Rule 1: The "líng" Rule (x0x)

In hundreds where the digit 0 is in the middle, the word líng (zero) MUST be pronounced! Note: Only middle zeros require líng.

101 yìbǎi líng yī
102 yìbǎi líng èr
109 yìbǎi líng jiǔ
304 sānbǎi líng sì
506 wǔbǎi líng liù
708 qībǎi líng bā
1023 yìqiān líng èrshísān
4506 sìqiān wǔbǎi líng liù
7890 qīqiān bābǎi jiǔshí (Trailing 0 is not in the middle; no "líng")
Crucial Comparison: Chinese vs Malay Omission Pitfall!

In spoken Chinese, native speakers often drop the trailing unit: 120 → yìbǎi èr (leaving out "shí").
Warning for Malay speakers: In Malay, "seratus dua" means 102! But in Chinese:

  • 102 MUST be pronounced with "líng": yìbǎi líng èr
  • 120 with trailing unit dropped: yìbǎi èr

This is exactly why Rule 1 ("líng" rule) is mandatory — without pronouncing "líng" in 102, it will be mistaken for 120!

Rule 2: The "yī" Rule (x1x)

In hundreds where the tens digit is 1, the word yī (one) MUST be pronounced before "shí"!

111 yìbǎi yīshíyī
112 yìbǎi yīshí'èr
119 yìbǎi yīshíjiǔ
314 sānbǎi yīshísì
516 wǔbǎi yīshíliù
718 qībǎi yīshíbā
1123 yìqiān yìbǎi èrshísān
4516 sìqiān wǔbǎi yīshíliù
7891 qīqiān bābǎi jiǔshíyī
⚠️ Important Exception Note:

When numbers 11–19 stand alone (without hundreds in front):

❌ do NOT say "yīshíyī", "yīshí'èr"... ✅ Just say shíyī, shí'èr!

💡 Reason: Without a preceding hundreds digit, there is no ambiguity about which position "shí" belongs to!

💡 Post-Break Core Concept: The Chinese Universal Number Formula

Before diving into Days, Currency, and Time, remember this Golden Rule: In Chinese, whenever numbers appear, they almost ALWAYS follow the structure:

Number (Nu) + Measure Word / Unit (M) (+ Noun)
💰 Currency (Nu + Unit)
• sān kuài (RM 3)
• wǔ máo (50 cents)
• liù fēn (6 cents)
⏰ Time (Nu + Unit)
• bā diǎn shíyī fēn
  (8:11 / 8 o'clock 11 minutes)
📅 Date & Calendar (Nu + Unit)
• yī jiǔ jiǔ jiǔ nián (1999)
• sì yuè (April / Month 4)
• èrshí'èr hào (22nd)
🚨 The ONLY Major Exception: Days of the Week!

Why do we say xīngqīyī, xīngqī'èr (Unit + Nu) instead?
Reason: In Days of the Week, xīngqī (week) serves as a fixed sequence anchor. The numbers 1–6 function as ordinal tags (Day 1, Day 2...) rather than quantity!

  • xīngqīrì: Used in formal context, written Chinese, calendars, or official documents.

6. Currency & Price Format KLXHY pg 16

Key Principle: Cents are read separately!

Unlike English or Malay where cents are read as a whole two-digit number, Chinese breaks prices down strictly into 3 units: kuài (dollars/ringgit), máo (10-cent dimes), and fēn (1-cent units).

⚠️ Special Caution: "èr" vs. "liǎng" for Number "2" in Currency & Time

💡 Rules to Highlight for Students:
  • Currency (kuài, máo, fēn): Single 2s in currency units use liǎng (liǎng kuài, liǎng máo, liǎng fēn). Tens position uses èr (shí'èr kuài).

10. Wrap-up & Homework

Core Points Summary:

  • 0-10 & Culture: 4 is unlucky (sǐ), 8 is lucky (fā)! Remember 0-10 requires writing Hanzi.
  • The Two 2s: "èr" for numbers/math. "liǎng" for quantity/time hours.
  • Hundreds Rules: x0x (e.g., 105) uses "líng". x1x (e.g., 115) uses "yī".
  • Currency: kuài, máo, fēn. (RM 76.84 = qīshíliù kuài bā máo sì fēn). Cents are read separately.
  • Time: diǎn, fēn. (2:00 = liǎng diǎn).
